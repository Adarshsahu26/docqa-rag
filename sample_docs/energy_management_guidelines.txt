Plant Energy Management Guidelines (SAMPLE DOCUMENT - fictional data for demonstration only)

Purpose

These guidelines describe how the plant measures, controls and reports its electricity consumption. They aim to reduce energy cost and to avoid penalties from the utility.

Metering

Main incomers and all large motors are metered with smart meters that record consumption every 15 minutes. Meter data is uploaded to the energy dashboard each night. A meter that stops reporting for more than 24 hours is treated as faulty and raised as a work order.

Peak demand

The contracted peak demand limit is 4.2 MW. When the forecast demand exceeds 90 percent of the limit, the energy controller starts demand response. During demand response, non-critical HVAC loads are shed first, followed by air compressors and then batch heaters. Production lines are never shed without approval from the shift supervisor.

Power factor

The plant must maintain a power factor of at least 0.95. The utility charges a penalty when the monthly average power factor falls below 0.90. Capacitor banks switch in automatically when the power factor drops below 0.93.

Reviews and audits

The energy team reviews consumption against the budget every month. The key performance indicator is energy intensity, measured in kWh per tonne of product. A full energy audit is carried out every 3 years by an accredited auditor.
