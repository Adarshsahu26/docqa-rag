Lockout/Tagout Procedure (SAMPLE DOCUMENT - fictional data for demonstration only)

Purpose

This procedure protects workers from the unexpected start-up or energisation of equipment during maintenance and repair work. It applies to all electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and mechanical equipment on site.

Who may apply a lock

Only authorised employees who have completed lockout/tagout training may apply a personal lock. The training must be renewed every 12 months.

Standard steps

Step 1 - Notify all affected personnel before the equipment is shut down.
Step 2 - Shut the equipment down using the normal stopping procedure.
Step 3 - Isolate every energy source, including electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and stored mechanical energy.
Step 4 - Apply a personal lock and a danger tag to each isolation point. Each worker uses their own lock and keeps the only key.
Step 5 - Release or restrain stored energy, for example by bleeding pressure lines or grounding capacitors.
Step 6 - Verify isolation by attempting a start-up from the local control station and by testing with a voltage tester. Return the controls to the off position afterwards.

Group lockout

When more than one team works on the same equipment, a group lockbox is used. Each team leader places a lock on the lockbox, and every worker adds a personal lock to it.

Shift changes

If work continues across a shift change, the outgoing worker's lock may only be removed after the incoming worker has applied their own lock and the handover has been recorded.

Removing locks

Before locks are removed, the supervisor confirms that all tools have been cleared, guards are refitted and everyone is clear of the equipment. A lock may be removed by someone other than its owner only with written approval from the site manager, after at least 2 attempts to contact the owner.
